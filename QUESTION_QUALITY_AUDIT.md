# UCAT Question Bank Quality Audit

Audit date: 28 July 2026

## Scope and method

This audit covers every question in the seeded active bank defined by `database.py`: 204 questions across 41 linked passage/scenario/data sets and 63 standalone Decision Making items. It does not assess user-created database rows, which are outside the seed bank and must not be modified.

The review checked format, stimulus use, reasoning steps, information density, answerability from the supplied material, distractor plausibility, ambiguity, outside-knowledge dependence, explanation quality, timing suitability, and answer-position balance. Linked questions were also checked as sets for repeated evidence and repeated reasoning operations.

## Official benchmark

Primary sources, accessed 28 July 2026:

- [UCAT Test Format and Scoring](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/about-ucat/test-format-and-scoring/)
- [UCAT Question Tutorials](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/prepare/question-tutorials/)
- [Official Question Banks and Practice Tests](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/prepare/practice-tests/)

The Consortium states that its official practice questions and tests are representative of the live test. The interactive 2026 question banks were therefore used as the difficulty and presentation benchmark. Their content was inspected only for structural characteristics; no official passage, scenario, question, explanation, or distinctive fact pattern is reproduced here or in the rewritten bank.

Officially confirmed requirements:

| Subtest | Questions | Time | Confirmed formats and scoring |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Verbal Reasoning | 44 | 22 minutes | Eleven passages with four questions each; four-option single-best-answer and three-option True/False/Can't Tell; passage-only reasoning; 1 mark per question |
| Decision Making | 35 | 37 minutes | Four-option single-answer items and five-statement Yes/No tasks; the latter are worth 2 marks with 1 mark for a partially correct response |
| Quantitative Reasoning | 36 | 26 minutes | Five-option single-answer items, mostly in four-question data sets; 1 mark per question |
| Situational Judgement | 69 | 26 minutes | Up to six questions per scenario; four-point appropriateness or importance ratings, plus most/least-appropriate selection from three actions; partial credit is awarded for responses close to the key |

The current test has four subtests and no Abstract Reasoning. Cognitive scores are 300–900 per subtest and 900–2700 in total; SJT is reported separately in Bands 1–4.

## Characteristics observed in the official material

These are observations used as an authoring benchmark, not extra rules claimed by the Consortium.

### Verbal Reasoning

- The inspected passages were dense nonfiction of roughly 220–345 words, with four linked questions.
- Credited responses often depended on qualifiers, scope, comparison, chronology, or synthesis across separated sentences.
- Plausible distractors usually preserved part of the passage while changing strength, cause, population, or time period.
- True/False/Can't Tell items required a strict separation between contradiction and missing evidence.

### Decision Making

- The official bank identifies syllogisms, logical puzzles, recognising assumptions, interpreting information, Venn diagrams, and probabilistic/statistical reasoning.
- Five-statement tasks require each conclusion to be tested independently for necessity.
- Strong items require several constraints or pieces of evidence to be combined; surface word matching is insufficient.
- Argument evaluation turns on whether the argument is relevant, important, and directly addresses the issue—not whether it is agreeable.

### Quantitative Reasoning

- Each inspected item used five options and most belonged to a shared four-question graph, table, or scenario.
- Difficulty came mainly from selecting relevant values, handling units or baselines, and combining two or more manageable operations.
- Distractors corresponded to recognisable errors such as using the wrong denominator, reversing a percentage, or stopping after an intermediate calculation.

### Situational Judgement

- Scenarios were grounded in clinical or medical/dental training but did not require procedural knowledge.
- The bank uses appropriateness, importance, and most/least-appropriate formats.
- Adjacent ratings were separated by proportionality, immediacy, likely impact, and whether an action addressed the central concern.
- Good rationales identify the professional principle, the people affected, the immediate risk, and why the neighbouring rating is too strong or too weak.

#### Standalone DM per-item register

The table below records a separate assessment for every old standalone DM item. “Operations” describes what the item actually demanded, not what its difficulty label claimed. The compact failure codes are:

- **K** — solvable by keyword matching or one explicit premise;
- **S** — only one routine arithmetic/set operation;
- **D** — weak, implausible, or non-diagnostic distractors;
- **A** — answer-position or answer-wording cue;
- **I** — insufficient information density or constraint integration;
- **E** — explanation does not fully test every alternative or possible counterexample;
- **O** — outside or clinical framing adds knowledge load without adding reasoning;
- **F** — format/coverage problem, including argument items stored under the wrong topic.

All 63 were answerable as written and none was retained: even where the key was defensible, the item did not meet the combined official-format, reasoning-load, distractor, explanation, and timed-suitability benchmark.

## Rewrite strategy

### Verbal Reasoning

Create eleven original nonfiction passages, each with four questions. Four sets will use three-option T/F/CT and seven will use four-option MCQ. The implemented validation range is 240–360 words, covering the density seen in the inspected official examples. Each set will mix retrieval with scope, causation, chronology, comparison, viewpoint, and synthesis; no set will repeatedly mine one sentence.

### Decision Making

Reduce the active seeded bank to 35 questions. Use multi-step syllogisms, five-statement necessity tasks, constrained arrangements, three-set reasoning, probability/statistical evidence, interpreting information, and argument evaluation. Logical items will be checked by enumerating valid models where practical.

### Quantitative Reasoning

Replace all nine data sets with original tables, schedules, rates, prices, and operational scenarios. Medium and hard items will require two or more selection/operation steps while keeping arithmetic calculator-manageable. Every numerical key will be independently recomputed in the validator.

### Situational Judgement

Build 16 original UK medical or dental training scenarios totalling 69 questions. Use appropriateness, importance, and most/least-appropriate formats. Each scenario will contain genuine competing duties without assuming medical procedure knowledge. Rationales will name the principle, stakeholders, immediate risk, and why adjacent ratings or alternative actions are less defensible.

## Baseline conclusion

No existing seeded item is strong enough to retain unchanged against the stated benchmark. The main problem is not metadata: the stimuli, reasoning operations, distractors, answer balance, format coverage, and rationales all need substantive replacement. Superseded seeded rows will be retired rather than deleted so attempts remain referentially intact; if a new item has an exactly matching legacy stem, that row is updated in place. User-created questions will not be matched or modified.
